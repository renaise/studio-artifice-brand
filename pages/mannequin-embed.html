Studio Artifice · exploration 2 · the mannequin

One scale, two readings, in the round

The D1 study moved onto the wooden mannequin itself. The figure carries one temperature field: hot at the head and the heart, cooling through the trunk and arms to the hands and feet. The heat order is blue, cyan, lime, magenta, which is the film order, so the same field reads as a thermal image or as film thickness.

Reading it. Every point on the surface has a reading between 0 and 1. Thermal shows it through the original ramp. Film reads it as thickness on the Film Spectrum: blue at 222 nm, cyan at 275, lime at 308 and magenta at 398. Moving over the figure tempers it there (on a touch screen, press on it). Heat adds thickness, so the color walks on along the scale: in film it keeps going through the higher orders, and in thermal it climbs to magenta and clips to white past 440 nm. Heating sets how fast hovering adds thickness and cooling sets how fast it fades, so the two compete: a slow pass climbs to magenta, and lingering builds rings through the higher film orders. At zero it holds, which is the temper record from D1. Higher values turn it into the heat trace from the field study, which cools at 1.2 per second. Emissivity falloff lowers the reading where the surface turns away, the way a thermal camera sees grazing surfaces as colder. That gives the figure its form without any lighting, so hue still only reports a reading. The ground is the cold end of the same scale. Every surface gives off its heat, front and back, so the halo follows the figure as it turns, rises a little like warm air, and stays between black and Production blue under heavy grain. It brightens as tempering adds heat. Bloom spreads the figure's own colors past its contour under the same grain, so the edge frays the way it does in the original image. The readout names the reading and the band under the pointer.

Registration Scope

A printer's registration mark is a circle and a crosshair. So is a vectorscope graticule, and so is the brand screw, a circle with the cross cut out. Printers check four plates against these marks at the edge of every sheet. Here the four plates are the four phase colors, and the check is the scope's reading: where each color actually landed against its target.

Registration black. In print, the marks are printed in every plate at once. Stack the four phase colors as ink and they multiply to black (lime × magenta × cyan × blue ≈ #00011A). Stack them as light and they add to white. An image on system brings all four plates into register and the mark reads as one line; drift splits it into colored ghosts.

The four marks from the reference strip, each made to carry one function of the scope. Scope: the trace itself, inside the inner ring. Neutral: the filled disk grows with the share of grey pixels. Phases: the CMYK quadrants become the four phase colors, each filled by its share of the color that landed on target. Register: four plates, each shifted by how far its color drifted from target. Hover a mark to see its pixels in the image.

Readings

The image placed on a proof sheet with bleed. Crop marks sit at the trim corners; drag a corner of the trim to recrop. The four registration marks on the sides read only what is inside the trim, and the slug line under the sheet prints the verdict. Crop away the color and the Phases mark empties; crop into a drifted patch and the Register mark splits.

Plate offset is the vector from a target box to where that color actually landed, read straight off the scope. On the scope (right), the target boxes are drawn as trim marks and each arrow is one plate's drift. On the mark (left), the same drift moves that plate. Inside a box counts as in register.

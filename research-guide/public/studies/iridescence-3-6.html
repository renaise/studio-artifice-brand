Move around the mark to tilt it
 

Reading it. Optically variable ink changes colour with the viewing angle, so a copy can't reproduce it. Here the screw circle carries a soap film (n 1.33). The pointer sets the viewing angle: tilting shortens the light path through the film, so the hue walks toward blue. Switch to Static export and the film is gone, because anything printed or exported is the plain monochrome mark. If it shifts, it is the original. This puts hue on a mark, which the library's rule (marks are high contrast only) doesn't allow yet, so treat it as an exploration.

07 Color · Interference chart
0 nm
 
Legend · pick a phase to read it

Reading it. One thickness axis runs from 0 nm (black) through the four phase colours at their film thicknesses and on to the pale wash past 1 µm. A colour counts as a reading only once its order and scale are declared, so the chart prints both. Drag along the axis, or focus it and use the arrow keys (Shift for 10 nm steps). The axis is physical, so it always runs in film order. The legend defaults to Figma order, which stays canonical until the order is decided.

Click or tap to cut
 

Reading it. Interference only happens where two surfaces sit close together, so the colour lives in the gap and never on either layer. As layer B (IRL) slides over layer A (URL), the strip at its leading edge is a wedge of air between them, thickest at the edge. The gap closes as B lands, the bands sweep down through the film orders to black, and the seam disappears. Each cut runs in brand motion steps of 200 ms on the brand curve. Iridescence is how the brand moves between states, never a state.

Move to steer the lens
#000000
 

Reading it. A lens coating is a quarter-wave film (MgF2, n 1.38, about 110 nm on glass). Its only visible trace is a faint glint at the rim on the side facing the light, and its hue shifts along the arc as the angle grows. Moving the lens fast tilts it a little and shifts the hue too. Everything under the lens stays greyscale: colour marks the instrument, never the thing observed. The swatch shows the glint colour at the centre of the arc.

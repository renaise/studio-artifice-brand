Mannequin directions

The mannequin is already a thermal reading, so every direction here starts by turning the image back into a temperature field (each pixel's position on its own ramp) and then reading that field in a different way. The image ranks the four colors by heat: blue, cyan, lime, magenta. That is exactly the film order from the iridescence work. The original asset already uses the proposed order.

Move to add heat · click for a pulse
 

Reading it. The Shape Field with the noise replaced by the mannequin. Each cell takes the Brand 5 glyph whose ink coverage matches the reading, so the figure is drawn in blanks, crosses, monograms, screws and squares, and the glow around it becomes a fringe of crosses. Level and span work like a thermal camera's: level is the coldest reading that shows and span is how much range fills the ramp, so the same image can read as a solid silhouette or as just the head and heart. The pointer is a heat source and its trail cools over about a second, which is the heat trace idea from the thermal research. A little drifting noise stays in as the image's own grain. A Rive version bakes the mannequin in as a 64 × 96 grid, with an input for other figures.

Drag up or down to move the isotherm
 

Reading it. The figure is grey by default and only the pixels at or past the isotherm take color, each in the band its reading falls in. Hue never builds the picture, it reports a condition on a declared scale, which is the thermal research's way of keeping the brand monochrome. Pull the isotherm up and the color shrinks to the head and the heart. "One band only" colors just the band the isotherm sits in, which gives one phase color per image for phase-specific covers or section openers.

Lines of equal heat
 

Reading it. The same field traced as isotherms, the way a survey draws height. The result is pure vector line work, so the mannequin can travel where a photo can't: engraving, embossing, a single-color print, a line icon at small sizes, a loading state. In motion the levels step down over time, so rings leave the head and heart and travel out to the hands and feet. The four band isotherms (blue, cyan, lime, magenta) can take color while the rest stays grey. Copy the still as SVG and paste it into Figma to try it as an asset.

 
0.00 s
 

Reading it. A 7-second sequence for logo motion or the sizzle reel, built only from the field. It opens cold. The head and the heart ignite first (they are the two magenta hot spots in the original image), heat spreads to the limbs, an isotherm sweeps down once to declare the scale, then the body cools away and the heart's heat gathers into one A monogram. Every frame is the same glyph grid with one number changing, so it can run live in Rive or render out for video.

Drag on the figure to temper it
 

Reading it. Because the image's heat order and the film order are the same, the temperature field can be read as film thickness: blue at 222 nm, cyan at 275, lime at 308 and magenta at 398. Rendered through the Film Spectrum, the mannequin comes back almost as originally drawn, so one figure carries both the thermal story (a reading now) and the iridescence story (a record). Dragging tempers the figure: heat adds thickness that stays, the temper record idea, and the color walks on through the film orders. "Full film from 0 nm" shows what the cold glow does on a real film (it goes grey, gold and violet before it reaches blue), which is why the default keeps the floor black.

Hover a target
 

Reading it. The mannequin on the Brand Vectorscope from the color check work: the circle is the graticule, the plus is its axes, the squares are the four targets, and the trace is drawn with the glyph ramp. The trace runs in heat order, from near Production round past Application toward Strategy, with a separate arm reaching for Identity, but none of it lands in a target box. the original image uses softened versions of the four colors (sky blue, mint, pale lime, pink), not the brand hexes, so by the scope's strict test it is off system. That suits a photograph, and it suggests a rule: the original image is the picture, and wherever the colors have to be exact (the isotherm figure, the Rive field) the bands use the brand hexes.

Color logic

Four phases, four colors: lime for Strategy, magenta for Identity, cyan for Applications, blue for Production. The set spans print and screen, with three near the process inks and one at the screen’s blue primary, and together they balance to a neutral gray. Each color is a reading on a declared scale, taken from thin-film interference, and appears only next to that scale. The row of four, in phase order and checked against its lightness, is the reference strip for every other color in the system.

Read off the film scale, thinnest to thickest: Production, Applications, Strategy, Identity. Set as a strip, they always run in phase order, each over its own lightness.

The four phase colors, in a flattened, blurred radial gradient, sit on the greyscale ground as a decorative layer.

Origins

Studio Artifice starts every project by looking closely, measuring what it finds and checking the work against something known. The brand identity design comes from tools built for observation.

Thin-film interference↗The colors in a soap bubble come from how thin the film is at each spot. Each thickness shows its own color.
→
Thermal image reading↗A thermal image comes with a bar that reads each color’s temperature. Without the bar, the colors mean nothing.
→
SMPTE color bars↗Before a broadcast, stations send stripes of fixed colors. Anyone down the line can check their screen against them.
→
Registration marks↗Each ink is printed in its own pass, and a circle with a cross on every sheet shows whether the passes line up. Screens check color on the same circle and cross.
→

Voice

Write like an editor, not a copywriter: assume a peer, state rather than sell.

Do

  • Flat declarative sentences a reader could check or dispute.
  • Observation over declaration, material before concept.
  • Warmth comes from noticing, not adjectives.

Don't

  • ✕No exclamation points.
  • ✕No aphoristic punchlines.
  • ✕No reversals built for quoting.
